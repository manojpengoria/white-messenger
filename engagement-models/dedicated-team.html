    Engagement Model

    Dedicated Development Team

    A stable, cross-functional team that works on your product full time, learns your domain deeply and adapts as your priorities change.

    Discuss Your Project Compare All Models

    Overview

    Your product team, without the hiring overhead

    Products that keep evolving need continuity. A dedicated team gives you consistent people who accumulate knowledge of your users, codebase and business, which becomes more valuable with every iteration.

    You set the direction and priorities; we handle recruitment, team health, engineering practices and delivery management. The team works within your planning rhythm and tools, so it feels like an extension of your organisation.

    Suitable project types

    • Long-term product development with an evolving roadmap
    • SaaS platforms after an initial launch
    • Organisations building digital capability without in-house hiring
    • Programmes that need several disciplines working together

    Scope definition

    How scope is defined

    Scope is managed through a living product backlog rather than a fixed specification. Together we maintain a roadmap for the coming quarters and a detailed backlog for the next few iterations.

    Capacity, not a list of features, is the commercial unit. That makes it easy to respond to new information without renegotiating contracts each time priorities change.

    Team structure

    Who is involved

    • Delivery lead

      Coordinates planning, removes blockers and reports progress.

    • Software engineers

      Front-end, back-end or full-stack developers matched to your stack.

    • QA engineer

      Owns test strategy and automation for the team.

    • UI/UX designer

      Full or part time, depending on the product’s design needs.

    • Specialists as needed

      DevOps, data or AI engineers who join for specific phases.

    Collaboration model

    How we collaborate

    1. 1

      Shared planning

      Iteration planning and backlog refinement with your product owner.

    2. 2

      Regular demos

      Working software reviewed at the end of every iteration.

    3. 3

      Your tools

      The team works in your repositories, chat and project tools where preferred.

    4. 4

      Transparent reporting

      Velocity, quality and risk metrics shared openly.

    Responsibilities

    Who is responsible for what

    White Messenger

    • Recruiting, onboarding and retaining team members
    • Engineering standards, code review and quality practices
    • Delivery management and progress reporting
    • Knowledge continuity when team members change

    Your organisation

    • Product vision and backlog priorities
    • Timely feedback and acceptance of work
    • Access to domain experts, systems and environments
    • Decisions on scope and trade-offs

    Trade-offs

    Advantages and limitations

    Every model involves trade-offs. Understanding them helps you choose with confidence.

    Advantages

    • Deep product knowledge builds over time
    • Flexible priorities without contract changes
    • Predictable monthly capacity
    • Ability to scale the team up or down with notice

    Potential limitations

    • Requires active product ownership from your side
    • Less suitable for short, one-off projects
    • Total cost depends on how long the team is engaged
    • Benefits grow over months rather than weeks
